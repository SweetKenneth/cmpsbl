<h2>Who Uses the Substrate?</h2>

<div class="card">
<h3>1. AI-Native Applications</h3>
<p>Applications that need persistent memory, governed autonomy, and multi-provider routing as infrastructure — not as custom-built features.</p>
<p><strong>Example:</strong> A customer support platform where every interaction is remembered, the system learns from resolution patterns, and AI providers are swapped seamlessly.</p>
</div>

<div class="card">
<h3>2. Enterprise AI Deployments</h3>
<p>Organizations that need audit trails, bounded autonomy, and regulatory compliance for their AI systems.</p>
<p><strong>Example:</strong> A financial services firm deploying AI advisors that must demonstrate every decision was governed, logged, and reversible.</p>
</div>

<div class="card">
<h3>3. Multi-Agent Systems</h3>
<p>Teams of AI agents that need coordination, shared memory, and collective learning.</p>
<p><strong>Example:</strong> A research agency where multiple specialized agents collaborate on market analysis — sharing insights through dream pooling and coordinated through CORTEX.</p>
</div>

<div class="card">
<h3>4. AI Researchers</h3>
<p>Researchers studying autonomous AI evolution, cognitive architectures, and governance models.</p>
<p><strong>Example:</strong> A university lab using the substrate to study how self-improving systems behave under different governance constraints.</p>
</div>
