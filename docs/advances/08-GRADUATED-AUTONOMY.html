Three-Tier Graduated Autonomy Governance

Formal Safety Scaling for Self-Modifying AI Systems

Kenneth E. Sweet Jr. · ORCID: 0009-0001-4237-1243
PromptFluid® · CMPSBL.com
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18234909

Abstract. We present a formal graduated autonomy model for AI systems where human oversight scales inversely with demonstrated system maturity. The model defines three primary operational tiers — Manual, Supervised, and Autonomous — each with specific boundaries for what the system may do without approval. Circuit breakers automatically downgrade autonomy when anomalies are detected. To our knowledge, this represents the first production-deployed graduated autonomy framework with automatic tier adjustment in an AI system.
Disclaimer: The designation "novel contribution" reflects the author's assessment based on comprehensive review of publicly available AI systems literature and commercial platforms as of February 2026. The author welcomes identification of prior art.

1. The Autonomy Dilemma

AI systems face a binary choice in current architectures: either the human approves everything (safe but slow) or the system operates autonomously (fast but unsafe). This binary is inadequate for production systems that must balance operational efficiency with safety requirements.

2. The Three-Tier Model

TierHuman OversightPermitted ActionsRestrictions
ManualAll changes require approvalFull diff preview before applicationNo autonomous modifications of any kind
SupervisedAdditive changes auto-appliedNew files, new functions, documentationModifications and deletions require approval
AutonomousMost changes auto-appliedAdditive and localized modificationsCross-module, self-referential, and destructive changes always require approval

3. Circuit Breakers

The system monitors its own behavior against calibrated anomaly thresholds. When anomalies are detected — unexpected error rate increases, performance degradation, or behavioral drift — circuit breakers automatically downgrade the autonomy tier. This provides an automatic safety net that does not depend on human monitoring.

4. Invariant Safety Rules

Certain operations are restricted regardless of autonomy tier:

  • Self-referential modifications (changes to the governance logic itself) always require human approval
  • Destructive operations (deletion of data, removal of capabilities) always require human approval
  • Cross-module changes that affect multiple subsystems require elevated approval
  • Budget-exceeding operations require explicit authorization

5. Prior Art Assessment

AI safety research discusses alignment, containment, and oversight theoretically (Bostrom, 2014; Russell & Norvig, 2020). Role-based access control is standard in enterprise software. However, no prior system implements a production-ready graduated autonomy framework specifically designed for AI self-modification with automatic tier adjustment based on behavioral anomaly detection.

6. Significance

Graduated autonomy provides a practical solution to the oversight scaling problem. As the system demonstrates reliable behavior, human oversight decreases — but automatic safety nets ensure that autonomy is revoked the moment anomalies appear. This creates a trust-building framework suitable for enterprise and regulated environments.
