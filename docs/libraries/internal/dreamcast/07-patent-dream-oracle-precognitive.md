## Field of Invention

Software systems that derive forward-looking forecasts of an originating user's future behavior, mood, decision, or risk from sub-threshold cognitive resonance vectors, using deterministic statistical inference (Bayesian belief networks, Monte Carlo simulation, survival analysis) without invocation of any generative machine-learning model, and that return said forecasts — together with disclosed counterfactual conditions — to the originating user under a user-owned receipt scope.

---

## Why This Is a Continuation-in-Part (Not a Standalone)

This filing depends on the resonance vectors produced by the substrate of doc 06. Filing as a continuation-in-part:
- Preserves the earlier priority date for shared subject matter
- Adds the precognitive forecasting subject matter as new matter under a fresh priority date
- Allows independent licensing of the reflective and precognitive layers
- Strengthens the family against design-around attempts

## Independent Claim 1

A computer-implemented method comprising:

1. obtaining a resonance vector produced by a deterministic sub-threshold cognitive resonance kernel as recited in [reference to parent claim 1];
2. providing the resonance vector as a feature input to a deterministic statistical predictor selected from the group consisting of: a Bayesian belief network, a Monte Carlo simulation, and a survival-analysis estimator;
3. computing, by said deterministic predictor and without invocation of any generative machine-learning model, a forecast comprising a target outcome, a point estimate, a confidence interval, and a horizon;
4. computing a counterfactual disclosure comprising an identification of one or more resonance components which, if inverted, would alter the forecast by more than a configurable threshold;
5. rendering the forecast and the counterfactual disclosure to the originating user via deterministic template substitution;
6. emitting a cryptographic receipt comprising at least: an identifier of the resonance kernel version, the predictor version, the random seed used in any stochastic predictor, the input feature hash, the output hash, and a digital signature; and
7. persisting the receipt under a user-owned access scope.

## Dependent Claims

- **2.** The method of claim 1, wherein the predictor is reproducible such that identical inputs and identical seed yield byte-identical output.
- **3.** The method of claim 1, wherein the forecast horizon is hard-capped at a configured maximum unless an elevated-consent state is recorded for the user.
- **4.** The method of claim 1, wherein the rendered output is restricted from disclosure to any party other than the originating user absent per-forecast explicit consent.
- **5.** The method of claim 1, further comprising: detecting that the forecast target is among a configured set of high-risk targets and routing the rendering to a pre-authored crisis-response template in lieu of the raw forecast.
- **6.** The method of claim 1, further comprising: capturing an actual outcome at or after the forecast horizon and updating a user-visible calibration history.
- **7.** The method of claim 1, wherein the counterfactual disclosure is itself committed to the cryptographic receipt.
- **8.** The method of claim 1, wherein the predictor consumes the resonance vector exclusively, without consuming demographic, biometric, or third-party-derived features.
- **9.** A non-transitory computer-readable medium storing instructions that, when executed, perform the method of claim 1.
- **10.** A system comprising a processor and memory configured to perform the method of claim 1.

## Strategic Notes

- **File 30–60 days after doc 06.** This preserves the parent priority for shared subject matter while creating a clean record of precognitive subject matter as new.
- **The counterfactual claim is the killer.** Most prior-art prediction systems do not disclose the conditions under which their prediction would change. This is both the legal defense and the patent's bite.
- **Hard-cap horizon as a structural limitation.** This makes the system distinguishable from forensic / surveillance prediction systems and gives examiners an easy reason to allow.
- **The recursion claim (claim 11) deserves its own filing.** Recursive self-prediction is genuinely novel and would benefit from a clean record.
- **Consider a defensive publication of "precognitive ensemble"** — multiple predictors voting on the forecast — to block competitors from designing around with ensembles.

## Counsel Brief (one paragraph)

> This continuation-in-part protects a software substrate that consumes resonance vectors produced by the parent filing as inputs to deterministic statistical predictors (Bayesian, Monte Carlo, survival), produces forecasts of the originating user's future behavior together with counterfactual disclosure of the conditions under which the forecast would change, and emits cryptographic provenance receipts. The novelty is the combination of zero-AI determinism, pre-conscious feature input, mandatory counterfactual disclosure, and user-owned receipts. No prior art combines these elements; the closest art (third-party churn-prediction systems) lacks the user-ownership, counterfactual, and determinism properties. A separate independent filing is recommended for the recursive precognition claim (claim 11).
