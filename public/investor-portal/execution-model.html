Productization Strategy

CMPSBL productizes as infrastructure, not as an end-user application. The system is consumed by developers, technical teams, and organizations building AI-powered products — not by consumers directly.

This positioning deliberately avoids the crowded consumer AI market and instead targets the persistent, high-retention infrastructure layer where customer relationships are measured in years, not sessions.

Three product surfaces serve distinct buyer profiles:

  • Master Substrate — The full cognitive runtime, deployed as a managed service. Subscription-based. Primary revenue driver.
  • Module Marketplace — Third-party and first-party cognitive modules that extend the substrate's capabilities. Revenue-share model.
  • Professional Services — Implementation support, custom module development, and architectural consulting for enterprise deployments.

Ecosystem Layering

The product strategy follows a deliberate layering model designed to build defensibility over time:

Layer 1 — Runtime

The substrate itself. Persistent cognitive runtime with governance, memory, and evolution. This is the core product and the primary source of recurring revenue.

Layer 2 — Modules

Composable cognitive capabilities that run on the substrate. First-party modules ship with the platform. Third-party modules are distributed through the marketplace. Revenue share creates ecosystem incentives.

Layer 3 — Templates

Pre-configured substrate deployments for specific use cases — customer support automation, research assistants, compliance monitoring. Templates accelerate adoption and reduce time-to-value for new customers.

Layer 4 — Data Network

Opt-in shared learning across deployments. Agencies that participate in dream pooling contribute to and benefit from collective pattern recognition. This layer creates compounding value — each new deployment makes the network more capable.

Distribution Strategy

Distribution is developer-first, enterprise-second:

  • Developer adoption — Free tier with meaningful capability. Documentation-driven. No sales friction for initial adoption.
  • Team expansion — Self-serve upgrade path to Architect tier. Usage-based triggers for upgrade conversations.
  • Enterprise engagement — Direct sales for Enterprise tier. Driven by compliance requirements, SLA needs, and governance demands that naturally emerge at organizational scale.

This model is designed to produce bottom-up adoption with top-down expansion — developers adopt the Creator tier, teams upgrade to Architect as projects mature, and enterprise procurement engages when governance and compliance requirements emerge.
